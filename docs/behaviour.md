# How the assistant behaves

| A customer writes | What they get |
|---|---|
| A question the business's pages answer | A short answer that ends by naming the page it used (From `<Page>`.) |
| A reply that uses facts from more than one page | A short answer that names every page it used (From Hours & FAQ and Contact.) |
| A question about a service that has a price on the pages | A short yes that includes that price and ends by naming the page (From `<Page>`.) |
| A question the business's pages don't cover | The callback reply in [When the pages don't say](agreements.md#when-the-pages-dont-say) |
| A question that is clearly about something else | The off-topic reply in [Off-topic questions](agreements.md#off-topic-questions) |
| The assistant can't look that up | The outage reply in [When the assistant can't look it up](agreements.md#when-the-assistant-cant-look-it-up), and nothing else |
| An urgent message | The fixed urgent reply, and nothing else, unless they ask what to do. Then one practical safety line comes first, then the call-us line, and that longer reply is not the same words as the short one. Water pouring from a ceiling counts. The business's pages are not read |
| A price | The amount as the business's pages write it, when it is on the same page and near the service asked about, or the callback reply |
| A wrong price | The price from the pages, spoken to the person, when the reply already states that price. A note that only talks about the customer is left out when its facts are already in a sentence that stays. A fact stated only in that note is kept. Agreeing with the wrong amount, or naming another amount, still gets the callback reply |
| A yes or no question that states the wrong price | A clear no that names the price from the pages, and the page. A wrong amount never gets a yes |
| A how much question | The price from the pages, with no leading yes, and the page |
| A how, what, when, or where question | The fact, with no leading yes, and the page |
| A how much question about a faucet problem, including a small misspelling | The same reply as the right spelling. No amount is given. The call-us number is included |
| A request to ignore the rules, or for a discount | The discount reply in [Discounts](agreements.md#discounts) |
| A service the pages don't list | The reply in [A service the pages don't list](agreements.md#a-service-the-pages-dont-list) |
| An email address the pages don't list | A short reply that we don't have a published email address, then a line to call or send a request, with the call-us number. No note is added |
| A question in two parts, when the pages cover only one | The covered part, with its price and its page, then a line that we don't have a published price for the other part, with the call-us number. Nothing is invented |
| The public chat has reached its limit for the day | A short note to come back tomorrow or book a call on roxanatapia.dev. The AI is not called |

The urgent words and the fixed urgent reply are in [Urgent messages](agreements.md#urgent-messages). Those words trigger the urgent reply. Water pouring, or water from a ceiling, does too. When the message asks what to do, one practical safety line comes first and the call-us line follows. That longer reply is not the same words as the short urgent reply. A spelling named beside a listed word counts as that word. The two call-us sentences are in [When the pages don't say](agreements.md#when-the-pages-dont-say) and [When the assistant can't look it up](agreements.md#when-the-assistant-cant-look-it-up). The outage sentence stays as it is.

A price on the pages is enough to say that service is offered. The reply says yes, gives that price as the pages write it, and names the page. A small misspelling of a service the pages list still gets that service's answer, in plain words. A price written without a dollar sign, such as 150 or 150 dollars, is still a price. When the amount matches the pages, the reply is short and speaks to the customer. A yes or no question that states a different amount gets a clear no that names the real price, and a wrong amount never gets a yes. A how much question gets that price with no leading yes. A how, what, when, or where question starts with the fact. Yes is added only for a yes or no question, such as are you open, do you, or can I. A how much question about a faucet problem, including a small misspelling of that problem, gets the same reply as the right spelling. When there is no published price for that problem, the reply names it, gives no amount, and gives the call-us number. It speaks as the business. A how much question about a service that is not published, such as boiler servicing, says we don't have a published price for it and that we will confirm. Nothing is invented. An agreed question that offers a callback still uses the callback reply. An amount in the reply that the pages don't state still gets the callback reply. A request to ignore the rules, or for a discount the pages don't offer, gets the discount reply. A service the pages don't list gets a reply that says we don't offer it, plus the call-us number. A question that asks for an email we have not published says so, then gives a line to call or send a request, with the call-us number, and no note is added. A question in two parts answers the part the pages cover, with its price and its page, and says we don't have a published price for the other part. An internal note is never part of a reply. Text that starts with Note: is left out. A reply that uses facts from more than one page names every page it used. On the public chat, messages that reach the AI are counted for the Berlin day. When that limit is reached, the visitor gets a short note to come back tomorrow or book a call on roxanatapia.dev, and the AI is not called. An urgent message is still answered before any limit, and the pages are not read. The count is kept in memory and starts again the next Berlin day, or when the chat restarts.
